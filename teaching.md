<section class="page-hero teaching-hero">
  <p class="kicker">04 / Teaching & mentoring</p>
  <h1>Build intuition.<br>Make the mathematics <em>visible.</em></h1>
  <p>I connect statistical theory, reproducible computation, and authentic scientific questions. Students should understand not only how a method works, but when it is appropriate, which assumptions matter, and how to communicate its conclusions responsibly.</p>
</section>

<section class="principle-list">
  <article><span>01</span><div><h2>Concept before procedure</h2><p>Visual explanations, simulations, and application-driven questions create a foundation for rigorous mathematical development. Once students see the statistical problem clearly, formulas become tools rather than isolated rules.</p></div></article>
  <article><span>02</span><div><h2>Reproducible computation</h2><p>Students learn to connect statistical reasoning with transparent code, diagnostic checks, and verifiable analysis. Computation supports—not replaces—the underlying reasoning.</p></div></article>
  <article><span>03</span><div><h2>Assumptions made explicit</h2><p>Every method answers a question under assumptions. I emphasize recognizing those assumptions, checking their plausibility, and explaining the consequences when they are imperfect.</p></div></article>
  <article><span>04</span><div><h2>Research through collaboration</h2><p>Mentoring emphasizes intellectual ownership, careful questions, clear writing, and respectful interdisciplinary teamwork. Students develop both technical independence and the ability to contribute within a research group.</p></div></article>
</section>

<section class="quote-band"><blockquote>“Statistics becomes useful when students can see the connection between a mathematical idea, a computational decision, and the scientific question.”</blockquote></section>
